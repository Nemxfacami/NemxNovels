Sourthen Ravenport



It was morning.

Nathan Mercer lay on his bed, staring at the ceiling as thin streams of sunlight slipped through the curtains, painting pale lines across the room. Dust drifted lazily through the light, the kind of movement you only noticed when you had nothing else to look at.

It was one of those mornings.

The kind where you slept far longer than you normally would—not because you were tired, but because you hoped that if you closed your eyes again, reality would wait outside the door for a little longer.

Nathan had already lost that battle.

Sleep had abandoned him nearly an hour ago, leaving him trapped with nothing but silence and a ceiling he had probably looked at a thousand times before. Funny how an empty mind could suddenly notice every tiny crack, every uneven patch of paint, every corner where the plaster met the walls.

He let out a quiet sigh.

"This ceiling makes me miss my job."

The words escaped without him realizing he had spoken them aloud.

There was something strange about him. Most people counted down the days until they could rest. Nathan counted the hours until he could return to work. Listening to broken people, watching them fight battles nobody else could see, trying to help them carry pain they had hidden for years—that was the only part of life that ever felt real to him.

When he wasn't working...

Everything seemed painfully still.

His eyes drifted toward the small table beside his bed. A plate with several cigarettes rested there beside his phone. He stared at them for a moment before reaching for neither.

Instead—

His phone began to ring.

Nathan glanced at the screen.

Michael "The Rat."

A faint smile almost appeared before disappearing just as quickly.

He let the phone ring for another second, then another, taking his time before finally answering.

"Are you still sleeping at this time?" Michael asked.

"Why would I be?" Nathan replied.

"I don't know. Maybe you're enjoying life while the rest of us are actually working."

Nathan chuckled.

"Says the hospital doctor."

A brief silence followed.

"So... why'd you call?"

Michael's voice became slightly more serious.

"Boss made the decision."

Nathan sat up.

"They chose you to become the new therapist at Rapha Asylum. You were supposed to report tomorrow, so start packing your bags and get ready to leave Southern Ravenport."

Nathan didn't answer immediately.

The room suddenly felt different.

The ceiling.

The silence.

The cigarettes.

The weight that had been sitting on his chest all morning.

For the first time that day, none of it mattered.

A small smile slowly spread across his face.

"I'll be doing that today, Mike."

Before Michael could respond, Nathan ended the call.

He stood up almost immediately.

The same room that had felt suffocating only moments earlier suddenly felt too small to keep him inside.

Rapha Asylum.

He didn't know exactly what waited for him there.

But for the first time in a long while...

He finally had somewhere he wanted to be.










The bus rolled steadily through the streets of Northern Ravenport, its engine humming beneath the quiet conversations of its passengers. Outside the windows, the city unfolded with remarkable order. Towering glass buildings reflected the afternoon sun, while rows of carefully maintained trees lined the broad pavements. People crossed intersections with quiet purpose, each seemingly following a destination already decided long before the day had begun.

Nathan Mercer glanced around.

The bus was filled with people from different professions. Across the aisle sat a woman dressed in a navy business suit, reviewing what looked like financial reports with a pen tucked neatly behind her ear. A few rows ahead, two engineers quietly discussed blueprints displayed on a tablet, occasionally pointing toward diagrams neither seemed willing to leave unfinished. Beside Nathan sat a scientist, completely absorbed in a thick book titled Chemical Combustion. Every now and then he adjusted his glasses before calmly turning another page, never once looking through the window.

It was strange.

The bus wasn't silent.

People spoke.

Phones rang.

Someone laughed quietly near the back.

Yet everything carried a sense of discipline, as though everyone understood there was a time for conversation and a time for work.

Nathan smiled faintly to himself.

Not once during the journey had he seen a teenager.

Not a child.

Not even a student carrying a backpack.

Everyone looked as though they had somewhere important to be.

Northern Ravenport...

The rock of production.

A city where purpose seemed to outweigh leisure.

As the bus continued, Nathan found himself comparing it to Southern Ravenport.

There, the streets breathed survival. Small shops crowded broken sidewalks. Vendors called out to passing strangers, children chased footballs between parked cars, and music drifted from open windows regardless of the hour.

Northern Ravenport breathed differently.

The streets were cleaner.

The buildings stood taller.

Even the traffic moved with quiet precision, flowing through the city without the impatience Nathan had grown accustomed to.

It wasn't better.

It was simply...

different.

The bus gradually slowed before releasing a long hiss as its doors folded open.

"Mason Street," the driver announced.

Nathan stepped onto the pavement, adjusting the strap of his briefcase.

A cool breeze drifted through the street, carrying the scent of freshly watered gardens mixed with polished concrete still warm beneath the afternoon sun.

For a brief moment, he simply stood there.

His eyes wandered across Mason Street.

Office towers overlooked research laboratories whose glass facades shimmered beneath the sunlight. Hospitals stood beside corporate headquarters, while universities and medical institutions blended naturally into the district as though knowledge itself had become part of the city's architecture.

People walked past carrying identification badges instead of shopping bags.

Conversations revolved around meetings, research, deadlines and patients.

The district didn't merely encourage work.

It seemed to exist because of it.

Several metres away stood a man in a neatly pressed white uniform.

He wasn't looking around for someone.

He was looking directly at Nathan.

The moment their eyes met, Nathan knew.

He's waiting for me.

"Afternoon, Dr. Mercer," the man replied with a polite nod. "I'm Evan Stains, one of Rapha Asylum's orderlies."

Nathan offered his hand, and Evan shook it firmly.

"Allow me."

Before Nathan could object, Evan gently took one of his briefcases.

As they began walking, Nathan found himself taking in everything around him.

The streets surrounding Mason Street felt unlike anywhere else in Southern Ravenport. Glass office buildings stretched toward the sky, their polished windows reflecting the afternoon sun. Laboratories, research centres, hospitals, law firms and corporate headquarters stood side by side, each building carrying the quiet confidence of people who had devoted their lives to a profession.

It felt less like a district...

...and more like an ecosystem built entirely around purpose.

Nathan realized he had been staring.

"Don't think too much about it," Evan said without slowing his pace.

Nathan looked at him.

"This is exactly where you'd wish to be."

"You think so?" Nathan asked.

"I don't think," Evan replied with a faint smile.

"I simply see it that way."

Nathan couldn't help but smirk.

Evan chuckled softly.

"'A long time' doesn't begin to measure the years I've spent here."

There was no pride in his voice.

Only certainty.

"I imagine a man in your profession understands what people mean when they say..."

Evan paused.

"'Chase what you love if you want to enjoy life.'"

Nathan looked at him for a moment but said nothing.

For reasons he couldn't quite explain...

Evan had his full attention.

Without realizing it, they had already reached the entrance to Rapha Asylum.

Only now did Nathan truly stop to look.

The asylum rose before him with quiet elegance.

It wasn't the dark, crumbling institution people imagined when they heard the word asylum. Cream-coloured stone walls stood proudly beneath a slate roof, while wide glass windows welcomed the afternoon light instead of hiding behind iron bars. Carefully maintained gardens framed the entrance, and tall oak trees stretched their branches across stone walkways that wound peacefully through the grounds.

The building felt old.

But not forgotten.

It carried the dignity of a place that had been cared for for generations.

Nathan turned to look behind them.

Only then did he notice they had already passed through the security gates.

Two uniformed guards remained at their posts beside the wrought-iron entrance, calmly observing everyone who entered or left. Neither had stopped them. A simple nod from Evan had apparently been enough.

Nathan couldn't even remember walking past them.

His entire focus had been on the conversation.

Interesting...

He watched Evan carefully place the briefcase beside the entrance.

"I should return to my duties now," Evan said politely.

"It was a pleasure meeting you, Dr. Mercer."

"You too."

Evan gave a courteous nod before disappearing deeper into the grounds of the asylum.

Nathan remained where he was.

Before him stood the enormous entrance doors, crafted from polished oak with brass handles that had clearly been touched by thousands before him. Above them, carved neatly into pale stone, were two words.

RAPHA ASYLUM.

He wasn't hesitating.

He was taking it all in.

It had been a long time since anyone had called him back to the work that made him feel alive.

Now...

He was here.

"You seem curious about who I am."

A brief silence.

"Don't be bothered."

Only then did he turn around.

He looked older than Nathan had expected. Calm. Composed. There was nothing imposing about him, yet something about the way he watched made Nathan feel strangely exposed.

The man walked down from the stage.

"Do you know that even the mayor's daughter is here?"

Nathan blinked.

"No... I didn't, sir."

The man nodded as though Nathan's answer wasn't important.

"This place..." he said, looking around the empty hall, "...isn't an asylum."

He paused.

"It's a library with plenty of books to read."

Nathan frowned.

"I don't understand."

"You will."

The man smiled faintly.

"And you should read the books."

Nathan hesitated.

"...Are you the warden?"

"If you prefer me to be."

Without another word, the man turned and walked away.

Nathan followed.
Neither of them spoke as they crossed the quiet corridors until they reached the chapel.
It was empty.

Sunlight slipped through the stained-glass windows, colouring the wooden floor in soft shades of red and blue. The silence inside felt almost sacred.

The man stepped forward, knelt before the altar, and closed his eyes.

His prayer lasted only a moment.

When he stood again, he spoke without turning around.


"And then they saw me..."

A pause.

"I was like a beast."

Another.

"I was like a monster..."

His voice remained calm.

"Bathed in blood, they said."

Silence filled the chapel.

"But no..."

He finally looked at Nathan.

"I was just a humble servant of God."

Nathan stood frozen.

The chapel remained silent.
