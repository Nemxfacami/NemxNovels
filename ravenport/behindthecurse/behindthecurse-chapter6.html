"You'll see."

She descended the first step.

Bella looked at Beatrice one last time.

The old woman was already sitting comfortably beneath the shade, watching them.

Bella sighed.

Then followed Becca into the basement.

Bella and Becca sat across from each other on old wooden chairs.

Candles surrounded them.

They were placed along the floor, on small tables, and even on the stone ledges of the basement walls. Their flames moved gently with every small draft that passed through the room.

Behind them, the basement door remained closed.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

Bella looked around the room before finally looking back at her aunt.

"What you are being taught is the same thing Beth, your mother, me, and those before me were taught," Becca said. "It has been passed down through the family for generations."

Bella nodded.

"And I would love it if you asked questions," Becca continued. "Don't just sit there and listen because you think you're supposed to. Ask. It's better to know more than you're supposed to know than to know less than you need to."

"I understand."

Becca smiled slightly.

"Good."

Bella looked down at her hands.

"I've always noticed something about the family."

"What?"

"The names."

Becca waited.

"Almost everyone has a name that starts with B. Even Dad."

Becca nodded.

"You're right."

"Why?"

Becca leaned back slightly in her chair.

"Because of the curse."

Bella's expression changed.

"The curse requires the continuation of a particular spiritual lineage. That is why the Beaumont name and the traditions surrounding it are so important."

Bella listened carefully.

"Anyone who is not part of the family and tries to marry into it can die."

Bella's eyebrows raised.

"Can?"

"It has happened many times in the past."

"How?"

"A spirit."

Bella remained quiet.

"The spirit kills the person who isn't part of the family," Becca explained. "There were times when people tried to avoid the curse altogether. Some refused to marry. Others separated themselves from the family."

"And that worked?"

"Sometimes."

"Sometimes?"

"People who truly loved each other sometimes stayed together without marrying."

Bella frowned.

"And they were still killed?"

"Yes."

"Both of them?"

"No."

Becca shook her head.

"The person who wasn't part of the family."

Bella stared at her.

"So the spirit knows who belongs to the family?"

"Yes."

"And it just... kills the other person?"

Becca nodded.

Bella was silent for a moment.

"That sounds horrible."

"It is."

Bella looked toward the candles.

"Then how did Mom marry Dad?"

Becca smiled faintly.

"That's a good question."

"Because his name already starts with B?"

"No."

Bella looked at her.

"Then why?"

"Your father was an outsider."

Bella frowned.

"But he became part of the family."

"Exactly."

"How?"

"Your mother chose him."

"Mom chose Dad?"

"According to her heart."

Bella smiled slightly.

"That sounds like something Mom would do."

Becca smiled.

"She didn't choose him because the family told her to. She chose him because she loved him."

Bella nodded.

"But if he was an outsider, how was he allowed to marry her?"

"That is where the tradition comes in."

Becca folded her hands together.

"For someone outside the family to become part of it, there must be a traditional wedding."

"A wedding?"

"Yes. A proper traditional wedding performed according to the family's rules."

"And after that?"

"They are accepted."

"Just like that?"

"Not exactly."

Becca shook her head.

"They receive a new name."

Bella's eyes narrowed slightly.

"A new name?"

"And a new last name."

"So Dad became a Beaumont because of the wedding?"

"Yes."

Bella thought about it.

"That's actually not as complicated as I thought."

Becca gave her a look.

"Don't say that yet."

Bella laughed softly.

"Why?"

"Because you haven't heard everything."

Bella's smile faded slightly.

She looked at Becca again.

"Okay."

There was a brief silence.

Then Bella remembered something else.

"What about telepathy?"

Becca's expression became more serious.

"What about it?"

"If someone has a telepathic connection with someone they love..."

Bella hesitated.

"Does that happen because of the curse?"

Becca stared at her for a moment.

"Not ordinarily."

Bella noticed the way she said it.

"Not ordinarily?"

"It's not something that normally happens."

Bella nodded slowly.

"So it can happen?"

Becca didn't answer immediately.

Instead, she looked toward one of the candles.

"There are things about the curse that we don't completely understand."

Bella looked at her.

"But you've been taught all this."

"Yes."

"So how can you not understand it?"

"Because knowing the rules doesn't mean knowing why the rules exist."

Bella thought about that.

Then another question came to her.

"What happens if I have a child?"

Becca smiled.

"That's a very distant problem."

"I know."

Bella smiled back.

"But what if I do?"

"What about it?"

"What if the child's name doesn't start with B?"

Becca's expression changed.

"Now that's a tricky one."

"Why?"

"Because you can't simply give birth to a child and decide that the tradition doesn't apply to them."

Bella frowned.

"But it's my child."

"Yes."

"So I can name them whatever I want."

Becca looked at her.

"Within reason."

Bella stared.

"What does that mean?"

"The family would consider it an abomination."

Bella's smile disappeared.

"An abomination?"

"And unacceptable."

"Why?"

"Because the lineage must continue."

Bella leaned forward.

"And what happens if I refuse?"

Becca was quiet.

Bella waited.

"Have you ever heard of Raker Beaumont?"

Bella thought for a moment.

"No."

Becca looked directly at her.

"He was your grandfather."

Bella blinked.

"My grandfather?"

"Your mother's father."

Bella's expression changed.

"What happened to him?"

Becca took a slow breath.

"Raker Beaumont was the only person in the family who had a name that didn't begin with B."

Bella stared at her.

"What?"

"Raker."

Becca nodded.

"It doesn't begin with B."

Bella looked down.

Then back at her aunt.

"So what happened?"

Becca's face remained serious.

"He refused."

"Refused what?"

"The name."

Bella's eyes narrowed.

"He refused to change his name?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Becca looked toward the closed basement door.

"That is a story you will hear later."

Bella frowned.

"Why later?"

"Because you are still learning."

Becca turned her attention back to Bella.

"And some parts of this family history are better understood one piece at a time."

Bella didn't look satisfied.

"But he's my grandfather."

"I know."

"And he had a name that didn't start with B."

"Yes."

Bella stared at Becca.

"So why wasn't he killed?"

Becca didn't answer.

The candles continued to burn around them.

Bella waited.

For the first time since the conversation began, Becca seemed to be choosing her words carefully.

"Because," she finally said, "Raker Beaumont was never like the rest of us."
