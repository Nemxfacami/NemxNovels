Personality

Traits

  • Emotional: Beth openly displays her emotions, particularly when dealing with Eljin's apparent death.
  • Sensitive: She seems deeply affected by the strange events happening around the family.
  • Imaginative: Her attachment to her teddy bear and the way she treats it suggest a strong imaginative side.
  • Childlike: Beth's behavior around the teddy bear gives her an innocence that contrasts sharply with the darker Beaumont family.
  • Attached: She forms strong emotional connections to objects and people around her.
  • Obedient: Beth generally follows instructions from older family members.
  • Curious: She is willing to engage with strange family events even when she does not fully understand them.
  • Anxious: The increasingly supernatural circumstances surrounding her family appear to make her uncertain and frightened.
  • Trusting: Beth tends to accept what older Beaumonts tell her rather than immediately questioning it.


Flaws

  • Naive: Beth does not appear to understand the full danger surrounding the Beaumont family.
  • Emotionally vulnerable: Her attachment to Eljin makes her particularly susceptible to grief and distress.
  • Dependent: She appears to rely heavily on older family members for guidance.
  • Easily overwhelmed: The family's supernatural events can become difficult for her to process.
  • Limited understanding: Beth knows pieces of what is happening but does not appear to possess the deeper knowledge held by Beatrice or Becca.
  • Passive: She generally reacts to events rather than attempting to control them.

Relationships

  • Eljin — Eljin is one of Beth's most significant relationships.

    Beth becomes deeply emotional after Eljin's apparent death and is seen crying over him. Her attachment to the teddy bear demonstrates that Eljin is not merely an object to her.

    Later, Eljin is revealed to be capable of speaking, and his existence becomes even stranger when children discover that he can communicate.

    Beth's relationship with Eljin therefore sits somewhere between childhood attachment and genuine supernatural connection.
  • Bella Beaumont — Beth and Bella are members of the same Beaumont family.

    Bella observes Beth's behavior and notices how emotionally affected she becomes by Eljin's apparent death. Beth later warns Bella about what she should do during the ritual, telling her not to open her eyes because people might see things humans are not supposed to see.

    This makes Beth one of the people who understands at least some of the danger surrounding the ritual.
  • Bianca Beaumont — Bianca is one of the older family members who gives Beth instructions.

    When Bella and Lucas collapse during dinner, Bianca tells Beth to get ready to go to the family house. Beth follows the instruction rather than questioning the situation.

    Their relationship therefore reflects the hierarchy within the Beaumont family.
  • Benjamin Beaumont — Benjamin is another older Beaumont family member within Beth's household.

    Beth interacts with him as part of the family structure, although their personal relationship is not explored in depth.
  • Beatrice Beaumont — Beatrice has a particularly unusual interaction with Beth.

    She gives Beth an old teddy bear and tells her:

    “You shouldn't force your body to do the work of two souls.”

    The meaning of this statement is not fully explained, but it suggests that Beatrice knows something unusual about Beth's circumstances.
  • Bianca and Benjamin's Family — Beth exists within a household where ordinary family life and supernatural activity happen simultaneously. She is therefore surrounded by people who know considerably more about the Beaumont curse than she does.
  • Raker Beaumont — Beth is indirectly connected to Raker through the Beaumont family and its inherited history. Her generation exists under the consequences of the discoveries and decisions made by earlier Beaumonts.

Backstory

Beth's personal history and childhood are not established in detail.

She was born into the Beaumont family and grew up surrounded by the traditions and supernatural circumstances associated with the family curse.

Unlike the older Beaumonts, Beth does not appear to possess a complete understanding of the curse. She seems to occupy the position of someone who has inherited the consequences of the family's history without yet having access to all of its secrets.

One of the most important parts of Beth's life is her relationship with Eljin.

She becomes deeply upset after Eljin's apparent death, crying over him and treating him as something more than an ordinary teddy bear. The family does not necessarily respond to her grief with the same emotional intensity.

Beatrice later gives Beth an old teddy bear and makes the cryptic statement that Beth should not force her body to do the work of two souls. The significance of this remains unexplained, but the wording strongly suggests that there is something unusual about Beth's relationship with the supernatural.

Beth also demonstrates that she has been exposed to information about the family's rituals.

Before the Beaumont family performs the ritual involving Lucas, Beth warns Bella not to open her eyes. She tells her that people may see things humans are not supposed to see.

This implies that Beth understands enough about the ritual to know that witnessing it can be dangerous or disturbing.

When Lucas and Bella collapse during dinner, Bianca instructs Beth to prepare herself for the Beaumont family house. Beth follows the family members there, where she witnesses the strange gathering surrounding Bella and Lucas.

At the family house, Beth again acts as someone caught between ordinary childhood and supernatural family traditions. While adults prepare for the ritual, ordinary conversations continue upstairs, creating a bizarre contrast between everyday family life and the supernatural event happening below.

Beth is therefore one of the characters who illustrates how deeply the Beaumont curse has become normalized within the family. What would be horrifying to an outsider is treated as another family responsibility.
