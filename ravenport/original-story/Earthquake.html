A journalist—whose name has since been quietly removed from public records—claims to have uncovered what many dismissed as coincidence, and others feared to even consider. According to their findings, the earthquake that shook Ravenport just weeks ago was not a natural occurrence.

It was triggered.

Seismic readings, when re-examined, revealed a pattern—too precise, too localized. Not the chaotic rupture of shifting tectonic plates, but something deliberate. Something initiated.

The source?

A spacecraft.

Official reports state that the mayor’s recently launched vessel, "Elysium", was part of a scientific expedition—“a search for the wonders of space,” as the press release described it. A mission of knowledge. Of progress.

But the journalist’s investigation suggests otherwise.

Eyewitness accounts from the outskirts of the city describe a low, resonant hum in the hours before the quake. Animals grew restless. Electronics malfunctioned. The air itself felt… wrong. Then came the tremor—not spreading outward like a natural quake, but pulsing upward, as if something beneath—or beyond—had responded.

What makes this more unsettling is the design of the Elysium.

Leaked schematics—since scrubbed from all official archives—bear an uncanny resemblance to a vessel described in an old, obscure science fiction film: a deep-space ark, built not just for travel, but for survival in conditions humanity was never meant to endure. A ship designed for long-term isolation. Psychological strain. Containment.

Or perhaps… escape.

Why would a city mayor commission such a vessel?

And why did its launch coincide so precisely with a seismic event that should not have occurred?

Some theorize that the Elysium did not simply leave Earth.

They believe it activated something.

Others go further—suggesting the ship itself is not entirely human in origin. That its design wasn’t inspired by fiction, but that the film merely echoed something that already existed.

Something remembered.

Since the launch, the mayor has not made a public appearance. Official channels insist all is well. That the mission is proceeding as planned.

But deep within Ravenport’s network, fragments like this continue to surface—unfinished reports, corrupted files, testimonies cut short.

And always, the same question remains:

If the Elysium was built to search the stars…

Why did something answer from below?
