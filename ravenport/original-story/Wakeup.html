Something is wrong with yesterday.

That sentence sounds ridiculous to write. But after hours of reviewing logs, archives, and personal records, there is no clearer way to explain what is happening.

Across Ravenport—and increasingly around the world—people are reporting the same thing:

They cannot remember how they spent yesterday.

Not vaguely. Not partially.

Completely blank.

At first, this was dismissed as confusion following Mayor Livius’s controversial broadcast about the so-called *Great Erasal*. Social media often amplifies suggestion. Memory is fragile. People convince themselves of patterns that are not there.

But then the newsroom servers were checked.

And that’s when the problem stopped being psychological.

Yesterday does not exist in the **MESS Channel** archive.

Not missing files. Not corrupted entries.

Simply… absent.

The database timeline jumps directly from two days ago to today, as if the entire network—every report, every recorded broadcast, every field note—took a synchronized pause.

Which should be impossible.

News systems do not “forget.”

They store redundancies across multiple servers, multiple locations. Even if a failure occurred, fragments would remain somewhere in the system.

But according to the internal audit completed early this morning, there are **zero records generated globally during that time window**.

No headlines.
No weather reports.
No breaking alerts.
Nothing.

Even automated systems that run without human involvement show inactivity.

As if the world stopped producing information.

Or as if something prevented it from being recorded.

Naturally, the internet has already begun forming theories. The most common comparison being what people casually call a **“matrix glitch.”** A moment where reality stutters, resets, or overwrites itself.

But this event carries a different feeling.

Because people aren’t just noticing missing data.

They are noticing missing time.

One witness described it like this:

> “I woke up today and realized yesterday feels like a closed door in my head. I know it happened… but there’s nothing behind it.” Another report from a hospital staff member claims entire shifts cannot be recalled, despite clear physical evidence that work occurred—patients treated, equipment used, energy consumed.

And yet no one remembers doing it.

This leaves a possibility that many are reluctant to consider, especially after the mayor’s statement about the Great Erasal.

If something once erased humanity’s memory…

Who is to say it cannot happen again?

Or worse—

Who is to say it didn’t just happen yesterday?

Investigators are now examining whether this event coincides with unusual signals detected in orbit shortly after the launch of the *Elysium* vessel. No official confirmation exists yet, but internal sources claim a spike in unidentified transmissions occurred during the same period that the “missing day” supposedly took place.

Which raises an unsettling question.

Was yesterday removed…

or were we simply not meant to remember it?

For now, the story continues to develop.

But if you are reading this, ask yourself one thing:

Try to remember yesterday.

Not what you planned.
Not what should have happened.

What you actually did.

If you can.

Because more people each hour are realizing they can’t.

And that realization is spreading faster than any news report ever could.
