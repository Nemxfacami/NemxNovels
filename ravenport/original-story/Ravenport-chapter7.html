Zeke had finished his oats, but the meal didn’t really “end” anything in him. It just gave his hands something to do while his mind kept moving.

He reached for his phone almost automatically.

Samantha.

The call rang once, then cut into that flat, impersonal voice.

“The number you have dialed is not available at the moment.”

He stared at the screen like it had misbehaved on purpose. Tried again. Same result.

A small pause.

Then again.

Same voice.

It wasn’t even rejection anymore. It felt like absence had taken a shape and learned how to speak.

He opened Facebook next. Typed her name. Opened the chat.

His fingers hovered.

Then he wrote something. Deleted it. Wrote again.

Nothing felt like it could cross whatever distance had formed between them. Even sending a message felt like throwing words into a vacuum and watching them lose meaning mid-flight.

Galactical message to another planet.

He sent it anyway.

No typing indicator. No read receipt. No reply.

Just silence.

That was when the Observer in him started working without permission.

It didn’t feel emotional at first—it felt analytical. Like it was trying to model what was happening to reduce the uncertainty.

She’s offline. Or unreachable. Or avoiding. Or hurt. Or gone somewhere she didn’t say.

But the Modeler/Builder part didn’t stop there. It kept reconstructing scenarios, trying to fill the missing data:

If she’s not answering, maybe her phone is taken. Maybe she’s at home. Maybe she’s asleep. Maybe something happened. Maybe her mother—

That last thought tightened something in his chest.

And underneath all of it, quieter than everything else, the Protector came in—not dramatic, not loud. Just dulling things slightly, like turning the emotional volume down so it wouldn’t overflow.

So instead of panic, what came out was heaviness.

A slow, dragging loneliness that didn’t ask for attention—it just stayed.

He stood up.

“I’ll just go check,” he said quietly, as if saying it out loud made it more logical than emotional.

The walk to her place wasn’t really a walk. It was Zeke trying to outpace his own thoughts while they kept walking beside him anyway.

He imagined conversations as he went.

“Hey, I just wanted to see if you’re okay.”
“Did you eat today?”
“Are you safe?”


Then his mind corrected itself immediately after each one.

Too much. Too needy. Too intense.

So he revised them mid-walk, like editing a system that kept failing runtime checks.

But underneath the revisions, the real thought never changed:

I hope she’s okay.

And then, quieter:

I hope she still exists in the same way I remember her.

By the time he reached her place, the Observer had already flagged the pattern: too quiet.

No movement. No signs.

The door confirmed it.

Empty.

He stood there longer than he needed to, like waiting could force reality to update.

Nothing did.

He went to the beach next, because that’s where she went when she needed peace. That detail mattered—his mind held onto it like a stable variable in an unstable system.

But she wasn’t there either.

No trace.

No presence.

Just wind and distance.

On the walk back, he started talking to himself—not loudly, not like someone breaking down, but like someone trying to keep his thoughts from collapsing inward.

“It sucks,” he muttered, “how people can just disappear from your day like you never existed in theirs.”

A pause.

Then softer:

“Maybe she’s busy… I don’t really know.”

But even as he said it, the contradiction was already forming inside him.

Because “busy” didn’t explain the silence. Not fully.

And that’s where the emptiness started to take shape—not sharp, not dramatic, just a relational gap where something used to be.

*Does she think about me at all?*

The question didn’t feel like anger. It felt like disorientation.

Like a system checking for a connection that used to return a signal and now returned nothing.

When he got home, the quiet felt familiar in a different way. Not comforting this time—just neutral. Like the house wasn’t responding either.

He passed Amanda’s room.

The door was open.

That alone made him stop.

Something about open doors in silent houses always felt like unfinished sentences.

He stepped inside.

The room didn’t feel “wrong” immediately. It felt altered in a way he couldn’t place at first. Then he saw it—symbols on the wall.

His brain tried to classify them before reacting.

Pattern. Writing. Meaning. Intent.

But there was no immediate translation.

Just marks left behind.

He stared at them for a long moment.

And instead of panic or anger, what came first was that quiet internal shift—Zeke slipping into the Builder again.

Not to feel, but to fix.

Not to understand emotion, but to restore order.

He moved closer.

“…I’ll clean it,” he said, almost automatically.

Because when Samantha was absent from the world and the room held something unreadable, the only stable thing left was action.

Something to externalize.

Something to make the silence less loud.

Amanda walked through the afternoon streets of eastern Ravenport with the book pressed tightly against her chest. The city was alive in that ordinary way that made people forget strange things could exist at all. Vendors shouted across the sidewalks, arguing over fruit prices. A radio somewhere blasted old music through static. Children chased each other between apartment corridors while women leaned over balconies gossiping loud enough for half the street to hear.

The sun hung lazily above the city, warm and golden, reflecting against cracked windows and puddles left from yesterday’s rain.

Marin’s apartment building stood near the edge of Markileven Street, painted in faded yellow with clothes hanging from nearly every balcony. Amanda climbed the stairs, hearing laughter before she even reached the door.

She knocked twice.

The door swung open immediately.

“Amanda!” Marin almost squealed.

Before Amanda could react, Marin grabbed her wrist and pulled her inside with enough excitement to nearly make her trip.

“Girl, come in!”

The apartment smelled of perfume, cheap candles, and freshly ironed clothes. TikTok videos blasted from the television while dresses, makeup kits, shoes, and handbags were scattered across the couch like a hurricane had passed through. Sunlight streamed through the curtains, hitting Marin’s face and making her look brighter than usual.

Amanda paused for a second.

Marin looked... happy.

Not fake happy. Actually alive.

She wore oversized shorts and one of Zeke’s old hoodies, dancing absentmindedly while holding two dresses against herself in the mirror.

“So,” Marin said dramatically, turning toward Amanda, “important question. Red dress or black dress?”

Amanda blinked.

Marin didn’t even wait for an answer before tossing the red one onto the couch and picking up another.

“You came at the perfect time because I am currently preparing for a life-changing date with a very handsome man.”

Amanda couldn’t help smiling a little.

The room felt normal.

Warm.

Human.

For a moment, it almost pushed the strange thoughts out of her mind.

Almost.

“Um...” Amanda reached into her purse slowly. “I actually wanted to ask you something.”

Marin was too busy fixing her hair in the mirror to notice Amanda’s nervousness.

“What’s up?”

“You remember that day at the café?”

Marin nodded casually. “Yeah, obviously.”

Amanda pulled out the old book carefully.

The dark cover looked heavier somehow.

“Do you remember me carrying this?”

Marin finally turned.

“Hm?”

She walked closer, took the book directly from Amanda’s hands, flipping through the pages casually without any reaction at all.

“No,” Marin said simply. “You didn’t have a book.”

Amanda stared at her.

Marin handed it back without hesitation and returned to searching through clothes again.

“You sure?”

“Yeah.” Marin shrugged. “I would’ve noticed. That thing looks creepy as hell.”

Amanda lowered her eyes toward the book.

Her stomach tightened.

“So...” Marin suddenly smirked, leaning closer. “Why are you acting like you’re in a detective movie?”

Amanda forced a tiny laugh.

“Nothing. Forget it.”

But her mind was already racing.

If Marin didn’t remember the book...

Then why did Amanda remember holding it so clearly?

The TV kept playing cheerful videos while Marin continued rambling about her date, completely unaware of the storm building inside Amanda’s head.

“Well, I gotta go,” Amanda said abruptly.

“Huh? Already?”

“Yeah... I still have things to do.”

Marin waved lazily. “Fine. But later I’m telling you EVERYTHING that happens on this date.”

Amanda nodded weakly and left.

The moment the apartment door closed behind her, the noise and warmth disappeared.

Outside, Ravenport felt different again.

The wind had picked up slightly. Cars rolled past slowly. Somewhere in the distance, construction metal clanged against metal with hollow echoes.

Amanda walked absentmindedly down the street, still thinking about the book.

Then she saw him.

Zeke.

He was walking ahead on the opposite side of the road with his hands in his pockets, hood over his head despite the daylight. He moved fast, weaving through crowds without looking at anyone.

Amanda slowed.

Something about him felt wrong.

Or maybe secretive.

He crossed the street without warning and headed toward the older side of Ravenport where the bridges and drainage systems cut underneath the city.



Amanda hesitated only briefly before following him.

Zeke descended beneath one of the massive concrete bridges where dirty water trickled through channels below. Graffiti covered the walls in layers upon layers of faded paint. Empty bottles crunched beneath Amanda’s shoes as she followed at a distance.

Then she saw it.
A tunnel.

Hidden beneath the bridge.

The entrance looked less like something built by the city and more like something the city had forgotten existed. Rusted pipes lined the walls around it like exposed veins. Darkness sat inside the tunnel unnaturally still, swallowing the daylight only a few meters in.

Zeke entered without hesitation.

Amanda stopped at the entrance.

The air coming from inside felt cold and damp.

Like breathing air from underground graves.

She should’ve turned back.

Instead, she followed.

Her footsteps echoed endlessly as she moved deeper into the tunnels. Water dripped from overhead pipes in uneven rhythms. The deeper she went, the more the city sounds disappeared until only silence and distant metallic echoes remained.

The tunnels twisted constantly.

Left.

Right.

Down narrow passages.

Through intersections that looked identical.

Amanda kept her eyes locked on Zeke ahead—

until suddenly—

he was gone.

Amanda froze.

Her breathing hitched instantly.

The tunnel ahead was empty.

“Zeke?”

Nothing answered.

The darkness around her suddenly felt massive.

The realization hit her hard.

She had no idea how to get back out.

Her heartbeat quickened as she turned around frantically, trying to remember which direction she came from.

Then—

“YOU KNOW YOU MIGHT DIE JUST CAUSE YOU FOLLOWED ME WITHOUT MY CONSENT INTO THE ANUS OF THE DEVIL.”

Amanda nearly screamed.

Zeke stood behind her out of nowhere.

Completely silent.

Amanda grabbed her chest, already halfway prepared to meet God.

“WHAT THE HELL IS WRONG WITH YOU?!”

Zeke looked almost disappointed she hadn’t died from fear.

“Sorry,” Amanda muttered shakily. “I just... find you suspicious.”

“Join in my ass,” Zeke said, turning around again. “Amanda, geez. Keep your thoughts to yourself and follow me.”

Amanda glared at him but obeyed.

The deeper they traveled, the stranger the tunnels became.

Some passages looked older than the city itself. Others had symbols scratched into the walls. Old cables hung from ceilings like dead vines. Occasionally Amanda swore she heard movement somewhere far beyond the walls.

Eventually they reached a large metal door buried deep underground.

It looked absurdly out of place.

Zeke stepped forward and knocked sixteen times in a strange pattern.

Silence.

Then heavy locks shifted from the other side.

The door creaked open slowly.

A tall man stood there smiling.

“Zeke,” he said warmly. “Been a while. You here for some lovely Charingas?”

“Eh nah,” Zeke replied casually. “I’m here for information.”

The tall man’s eyes shifted toward Amanda standing behind him.

His smile widened slightly.

“I see you brought someone.”

His eyes lingered on her carefully.

“A friend... or a foe?”

Zeke pushed past him before Amanda could answer.

“Geez dude, chill. It’s just Amanda.”

Amanda stepped inside cautiously.

The room beyond looked like some underground bunker made by paranoid criminals and conspiracy theorists. Lamps flickered dimly above stacks of books, old radios, maps, strange devices, and piles of handwritten papers.

Two other men sat nearby playing cards.

Both immediately looked up at Amanda.

“Hello, Amanda,” the tall man said smoothly while still studying her.

“I’m Nezgad.”

Amanda nodded awkwardly before following Zeke deeper inside.

“I see KayJay and Kevin are still alive,” Zeke muttered.

Kevin snorted. “Got my eyes back.”

“Yeah that’s beautiful,” Zeke replied dryly.

Amanda glanced at Kevin instinctively.

One of his eyes looked slightly off.

Not fake.

Not damaged.

Just... wrong somehow.

Nezgad disappeared briefly into another room.

Amanda slowly approached the card table.

“What are you guys even doing down here?” she asked quietly.

“Surviving,” KayJay answered without looking up from his cards.

“And avoiding death,” Kevin added proudly.

“Mostly death,” KayJay corrected.

Nezgad returned carrying a thick book. He placed it heavily onto the table.

“So,” he said while opening it carefully, “we found several anomalies recently. Couldn’t capture most of them though.”

His eyes lifted toward Zeke.

“But one of them...”

The room suddenly felt quieter.

“...one of them is stranger than the others.”

Zeke leaned against the table slightly. A grin slowly appeared on his face.

“Now we’re talking.”



###Southern Ravenport

Rainwater dripped steadily from broken concrete above as Dr. Bennensmith descended deeper into the underground passageways beneath the city.

The deeper she went, the quieter Ravenport became.

No traffic.

No distant music.

No human voices.

Only the slow echo of water moving through old sewer channels beneath the forgotten districts of Southern Ravenport — the part of the city people pretended not to see unless they were forced into it.

Gang territory.

Robber dens.

Collapsed neighborhoods abandoned by ordinary law years ago.

Ironically, it was the safest place she knew.

A rusted metal gate stood at the end of the tunnel.

Dr. Bennensmith pressed her hand against a hidden scanner beside the wall.

A soft mechanical click followed.

The gate opened inward.

Warm artificial light spilled into the darkness.

Her sanctuary.

Unlike her apartment, this place carried no illusion of normal life. No decorative furniture. No carefully constructed comfort.

Only purpose.

Research papers covered entire walls. Continuity maps layered over one another with handwritten notes scribbled violently across their surfaces. Old Prometheus files sat stacked in sealed containers beside malfunctioning monitors that still flickered with fragmented data streams.

At the center of the room stood a massive table covered in photographs, symbols, and names connected by dark marker lines like the reconstruction of a reality too unstable to organize properly.

Dr. Bennensmith removed her gloves slowly.

Exhaustion settled visibly across her face now that no one was there to observe her.

She opened one of the nearby folders.

Inside—

old reports.

Failed containment attempts.

Memory-loss incidents.

Identity-collapse records.

And one repeated name appearing across multiple timelines of documentation.

Lethe Vestige.

Dr. Bennensmith stared at it quietly for several seconds.

Then sighed.

“I can’t believe the day has finally come,” she murmured.

Her voice echoed faintly through the underground chamber.

“The day Lethe becomes the end of my peace.”

She flipped through several pages carefully.

Some reports had been crossed out manually.

Others looked partially rewritten, as though the information itself had changed after being recorded.

“A threat larger than the Inspectors themselves…” she said softly.

Her eyes darkened slightly.

“A person who knows about the Great Erasal.”

Silence followed.

Only the low electrical hum of the monitors answered her.

Dr. Bennensmith leaned back against the table and closed her eyes briefly.

For the first time since leaving the apartment, she looked genuinely tired.

“Ahh… life.”

A faint, humorless smile touched her face.

Then it disappeared again.

“And now there’s Cecilia,” she added quietly.

“Another variable.”

Her gaze drifted toward one of the monitors nearby.

A district map of Ravenport flickered weakly across the screen.

Several regions were marked with instability indicators.

But one signal remained circled repeatedly in red.

Eastern Ravenport.

Dr. Bennensmith looked at it in silence.

Then slowly reached for another file.

This one had no label. No classification.

Only a single photograph attached to the front.

A young boy standing beside a woman whose face had been partially distorted beyond recognition.

Lethe.

For a moment, even Dr. Bennensmith hesitated before opening it.

Morning light drifted softly through the curtains of the small house as silence rested peacefully over the living room.

The picture frames had changed again.

Where empty spaces once existed beside Magra in old family photographs, Lethe now stood naturally within them — smiling faintly in moments that had supposedly happened years ago.

A birthday picture near the television.

A beach photograph hanging beside the hallway.

Even a faded school portrait resting on a shelf near the kitchen.

Nothing looked edited.

Nothing looked forced.

Reality had simply decided he belonged there.

Inside his room, Lethe slowly opened his eyes.

The remnants of a dream lingered faintly in his mind.

Fragments.

A distant shoreline.

Black water moving upward instead of downward.

Voices speaking from somewhere beneath the earth.

He could no longer remember the words clearly.

Only the feeling remained.

Peace.

For the first time in a long while, he woke without pressure crushing against his chest.

Without that strange sensation of existing incorrectly.

He sat up slowly and looked around the room.

Everything felt... stable.

Too stable.

Lethe quietly stood from the bed and stepped into the hallway.

The smell of breakfast drifted from the kitchen while the television played softly in the living room.

“Mornin’, Lethe,” Magra said casually from the couch, her eyes still fixed on the television. “Your food’s at the table.”

“Morning, Mom,” Lethe replied calmly.

The words came naturally now.

Not awkward.

Not uncertain.

As if they had always belonged there.

He entered the kitchen and sat at the table, staring briefly at the plate waiting for him.

Another small thing that should have felt normal.

Yet didn’t.

A knock echoed through the house.

Magra raised her voice slightly from the living room.

“Hey, can you check who’s at the door?”

“Alright.”

Lethe stood and walked toward the entrance.

When he opened the door, the elderly woman from next door stood outside holding a grocery bag against her hip.

“Morning, Miss Donne,” Lethe greeted politely.

Miss Donne blinked in surprise.

“Oh— dear, I’ve never seen you before. Is Magra home?”

“Yeah, she’s inside.”

Lethe stepped aside politely as she entered.

He closed the door behind her.

Then—

Miss Donne paused.

Her expression shifted subtly.

Not confusion.

Correction.

Like a misplaced memory quietly sliding back into its proper position.

Her eyes returned to Lethe.

“Aww, Lethe,” she said warmly, the earlier confusion already gone from her voice. “You’re back. Haven’t seen you in a while.”

Lethe remained still for a moment.

Then smiled faintly.

“Yeah,” he replied softly. “It’s nice to be back.”

Miss Donne smiled and continued into the living room as though nothing strange had happened at all.

She sat beside Magra on the couch.

“Has the show started yet?” she asked.

“You’re too early,” Magra laughed lightly. “It’s about to begin.”

The two women settled comfortably into their telenovela while familiar dramatic music filled the room.

Meanwhile, Lethe returned quietly to the kitchen.

He sat down again and continued eating in silence.

But his eyes drifted occasionally toward the hallway.

Toward the photographs.

Toward the house itself.

The world was adjusting around him faster now.

Not resisting.

Accepting.

And somehow—

that frightened him more than being forgotten ever did.

After finishing his meal, Lethe stood slowly and carried the empty plate to the sink.

“Mhm? Going somewhere?” Magra asked casually from the living room without looking away from the television.

“Yeah,” Lethe answered after a brief pause.

“There’s something I need to check.”

He grabbed his jacket from beside the door and stepped outside into the cool morning air.

The neighborhood looked ordinary.

Peaceful.

Children crossing streets.

Laundry moving gently in the wind.

People continuing their lives without interruption.

Yet beneath that normality, something felt wrong.

Not emotionally.

Structurally.

The dream from earlier still lingered in fragments inside his mind.

And somewhere within those fragments—

he had noticed something.

Something hidden.

Something that should not have existed.

Lethe adjusted his jacket slightly and began walking down the street alone.

Toward an answer he wasn’t sure he truly wanted to find.
