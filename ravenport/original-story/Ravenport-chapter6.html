Zeke woke to the soft chorus of birds outside his window, their songs slipping through the curtains like little messengers announcing that God had sustained him for yet another day. Morning light poured into the room in lazy golden streaks, touching the scattered books on his desk and the half-open Bible beside his bed. He had already finished his morning declarations — bold words spoken into the air with sleepy confidence — and now the house felt strangely still.

With a groan, he pushed himself off the bed, dragging his feet across the cold wooden floor before stopping at his desk. He stared down at the scribbled schedule for the day.

Not much to do, he thought.

Then suddenly — like lightning splitting a dark cloud — remembrance struck him.

Yesterday, Samantha never showed up at their usual meeting spot.

Zeke blinked once.

Maybe I should go check on her.

He slipped out of his room and headed downstairs, the old staircase creaking dramatically beneath every step as if narrating his movements to the entire house. Normally, Amanda would already be awake by now. She was always the first soul to rise — humming in the kitchen, yelling at a kettle, or acting like the self-appointed guardian of breakfast.

But today?

Silence.

No Amanda in the sitting room.

No Amanda in the kitchen.

Not even the sound of a spoon clinking against a cup.

That alone felt illegal.

Zeke frowned slightly before wandering toward her room. Without knocking — because boundaries were apparently optional in this household — he pushed the door open.

And froze.

The scene before him was so unexpected that his poor male brain nearly short-circuited.

Amanda...

...was sleeping.

Actually sleeping.

Not fake-sleeping.

Not “I’m awake but pretending to sleep so nobody bothers me” sleeping.

Real sleeping.

The curtains danced softly from the morning breeze, making strands of her hair move across the pillow like drifting ink. Blankets were twisted around her legs from what looked like a violent battle against dreams. The room smelled faintly of perfume, warm blankets, and the tragic remains of late-night snacks.

Zeke slowly approached her bed with the caution of a man inspecting an ancient cursed artifact.

“To think this creature rests...” he whispered in disbelief.

He leaned closer.

Maybe she had died.

Honestly, that explanation felt more believable.

Licking his lips nervously, he placed a hand on her shoulder, accidentally pushing part of the blanket aside in the process. His eyes widened slightly.

“Ah.”

He respectfully looked literally anywhere else.

Then he touched her neck carefully, checking for a pulse.

“Beautiful skin she has,” he thought with the seriousness of a scholar discovering forbidden knowledge.

A heartbeat.

She was alive.

“Hmmm,” Zeke muttered, relieved, removing his hand from her neck.

At that exact moment—

Amanda shot upright from the bed like a possessed Victorian child escaping an exorcism.

“HAAAH—!”

She rose so fast the blankets practically flew into the air. Sweat clung to her skin as she breathed heavily, looking like someone who had just crawled out of a river after fighting demons underwater.

Still trapped between dream and reality, she stared blankly ahead, not even noticing Zeke standing beside the bed.

Meanwhile, Zeke awkwardly glanced around the room, trying very hard to become spiritually invisible.

That was when he noticed it.

Under his foot.

A pink pair of panties.

His soul nearly left his body.

“Ehh… pink,” he muttered with the calmness of a man moments away from cardiac arrest. “I should be going.”

He slowly lifted his foot like he had stepped on a landmine.

Then immediately escaped the room with the speed and dignity of a fleeing raccoon.

Downstairs, the kitchen greeted him with warmth — sunlight reflecting off metal pots, the smell of bread lingering in the air, and the comforting crackle of an old stove waking up for duty.

Zeke exhaled dramatically.

“Ohhh, lovely oats,” he said, placing a hand over his chest. “My favourite.”

Anything to recover from what his eyes had just witnessed.

Darkness.

She turned on the light.

Lethe Vestige sat on her couch, motionless, one arm resting against the side of his face as though he had been waiting for hours.

She walked past him, pressing two fingers against her temple.

“Great. Now I have to abandon another apartment.”

“I needed to ask you something.”

“Then ask,” she replied flatly as she entered the kitchen.

The sound of a cabinet opening echoed softly.

The bookshelves.

The warm lights.

The untouched dinner plate beside the sink.

A life.

“You remember this place differently, don’t you?” Dr. Bennensmith asked without turning around.

Lethe looked toward her.

“This place isn’t real.”

A pause.

“Or maybe it is,” he continued quietly. “That’s the problem.”

“You’re remembering too much again.”

She finally turned toward him.

“Telling you won’t change anything.”

“You made copies of yourself just to live comfortably in Ravenport.”

“Human.”

Dr. Bennensmith walked over and sat beside him.

Closer now, she looked genuinely tired.

“Lethe… your ability to remember at all is an anomaly. Most people lose themselves completely once the process starts.”

He walked toward the door.

Dr. Bennensmith did not try to stop him.

The lock clicked shut behind him.

Silence returned.

### V corp rooftop

Cecilia stood motionless on the rooftop of V.corp while the cold wind drifted around her in slow, uneven currents.

Far below, Ravenport carried on as if nothing had changed.

Traffic crawled through rain-darkened streets. Neon lights flickered across glass windows. People moved from one destination to another, unaware that something unseen had begun moving through the city beside them.

Her eyes remained fixed on the location where the RIS had detected the anomaly only minutes earlier.

There was nothing there now.

No figure.

No movement.

Not even a recognizable trace.

Only a faint distortion lingering in the air like the afterimage of something that had never fully belonged to reality in the first place.

At her side, the RIS emitted a soft pulse.

> “Primary signal deteriorating.”

Cecilia narrowed her eyes slightly.

Every encounter had followed the same pattern.

The anomaly appeared.

Then vanished before direct observation could be maintained.

No stable visual record. No confirmed identity. Only fragments.

Her voice broke the silence, calm and measured.

> “Explain signal inconsistency.”

For a moment, only the sound of distant traffic answered her.

Then the RIS responded.

> “Direct anomaly tracking unsuccessful.”

Another pulse flickered across the interface.

> “Environmental continuity deviations remain after entity departure.”

Cecilia turned her gaze back toward the city skyline, thinking carefully.

The cemetery.

V.corp.

Different locations. Different circumstances.

Yet both shared the same underlying condition.

Not destruction.

Not corruption.

Alteration.

The anomaly was not merely appearing within environments.

It was changing them.

Places did not return to normal after contact. Something remained behind, woven subtly into reality itself.

Cecilia straightened slightly as the conclusion settled into place.

> “If direct presence cannot be maintained…”

She paused briefly.

> “Then residual alteration must be tracked instead.”

The RIS processed the statement immediately. Its interface flickered across her vision as streams of data unfolded into layered district maps and structural scans.

Occupancy irregularities.

Localized continuity fractures.

Architectural inconsistencies.

Most stabilized within seconds.

Corrected.

Erased.

Forgotten by the system.

But one signal remained.

Weak.

Persistent.

Eastern Ravenport.

Residential sector.

The RIS pulsed again.

> “Residual instability concentration detected.”

Cecilia stepped away from the rooftop edge and moved toward the stairwell exit.

> “Estimated time since entity presence: recent.”

She slowed for only a moment.

> “Can identity be confirmed?”

Silence followed.

Then—

> “Negative.”

A brief pause.

> “Continuity distortion interfering with classification.”

Cecilia said nothing more.

She descended through the dim stairwell and exited onto the streets below.

Rain had started by then. Light enough to be ignored, yet heavy enough to leave the pavement shining beneath the city lights. Passing vehicles sent reflections rippling through the wet roads as distant sirens echoed somewhere deeper within the city.

She moved through Ravenport without hesitation.

Past shuttered storefronts.

Empty sidewalks.

Flickering signs that buzzed faintly against the rain.

The RIS signal fluctuated constantly.

Stronger.

Then weaker.

Then stronger again.

Unstable.

Like something trying to exist while reality resisted it.

Eventually Cecilia stopped in front of an aging apartment complex tucked between two narrow side streets.

Old concrete walls.

Dim windows.

Recently inhabited.

She slowly lifted her gaze toward the upper floors.

The RIS pulsed sharply against her side.

> “Residual anomaly field confirmed.”

Another pause followed.

> “Entity no longer present.”

Rainwater slid quietly from the edge of the building as Cecilia remained still for several seconds, studying the structure in silence.

Watching.

Listening.

As if expecting the building itself to react to her presence.

Then, without another word, she stepped forward and entered the apartment complex.
