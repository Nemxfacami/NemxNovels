This was the moment he’d been waiting for — the instant before collapse, before instinct gave way, before people stopped pretending they were safe.

The Dark Child had arrived.

And the forest knew it was time to feed.

*they say the dark child doesn't see humans as threats because it believes we don't see it*

Said Zeke as he moved through the trees, his boots brushing wet leaves without urgency, without fear. He walked the way a man walks through a room he owns.

Around him, the group lay scattered across the forest floor — breathing, barely. Their fear still lingered in the air like smoke after a fire, and the Dark Child drank it slowly, patiently, the way something eternal eats.

The anomaly crouched low, its form pulsing faintly, darkness thickening and thinning as if it were inhaling emotion itself. Its eyes stayed fixed on the bodies — not hunting, not rushing — simply feeding.

*I am not a threat to it - I figured it's only seen by humans gifted with specific skills such as myself*

Zeke stepped out of the shadow.

Nothing happened.

No reaction. No flinch.

The Dark Child did not acknowledge him — because to it, Zeke was absence. A blind spot in reality. A thing outside its pattern of fear.

He kept walking.

Each step brought him closer to something that should have triggered instinct, terror, survival — but Zeke felt none of it. Only focus. Calculation.

The air around the anomaly felt wrong. Heavy. Pressurized. Like standing too close to something that bends the rules quietly.



Zeke stopped an arm’s length away.

The Dark Child shifted slightly, its body folding inward, dark energy oozing off it like condensation. For a brief second, its eyes flicked toward him — not in alarm, not in recognition — but in vague confusion.

Then it looked away.

Dismissed him.

Zeke smiled thinly.

In one smooth motion, he produced the syringe.

It was large. Industrial. Filled with a thick, lightless fluid that seemed to swallow what little moonlight touched it.

He plunged it forward.

The needle sank into the Dark Child’s mass with resistance — not flesh, not bone — more like piercing through congealed shadow. The anomaly convulsed violently, its limbs jerking, its entire form rippling as if reality itself rejected what was being injected into it.

It did not scream.

It could not.

Its eyes widened instead — brighter, frantic — finally seeing Zeke, not as a man, but as an error.

The ground beneath them trembled.

Darkness spilled outward in waves, choking the air, pressing down on the forest. Leaves lifted. Branches creaked. The bodies on the ground shuddered as if the fear inside them was being ripped away too fast.

The Dark Child thrashed, clawing at nothing, collapsing inward on itself as the substance spread through its form.

Zeke didn’t let go.

He went down with it, knee hitting the earth, hand locked around the syringe, teeth clenched — not in panic, not in effort — but in satisfaction he refused to acknowledge.

They hit the ground together.

The anomaly writhed once more… then stilled.

The forest exhaled.

Silence returned — heavier than before.

Zeke remained crouched over it for a moment, breathing evenly, watching the darkness settle like ash. The Dark Child lay inert, eyes dimmed but not closed — as if even subdued, it refused to fully leave.

Relentless.

That was the word.

Zeke stood, wiped his hand against his coat, and looked back toward the broken group on the forest floor — alive, traumatized, emptied.

The hunt wasn’t over.

It had only moved to its next phase.

Amanda pushed through the brush, branches snapping softly beneath her boots.

The smell hit first — iron, damp earth, something old and wrong. Then the shapes resolved in the dark.

Bodies.

Not dead — she could hear breathing, shallow and uneven — but broken, sprawled like discarded thoughts across the forest floor. And between them… it.

A thing crouched wrong against the earth, hair matted and thick, swallowing moonlight, its limbs folded inward like it had collapsed from exhaustion rather than injury. Its eyes were dim now, unfocused, but still there.

Amanda staggered back a step, hand flying to her mouth.

Zeke straightened slowly beside it, calm as ever, as if he’d just finished a chore.

“you came just in time to help me out with this Dark child of mine”

He flicked the syringe toward her without looking.

Instinct caught it before her mind could protest. The weight of it made her stomach drop.

“Chill you are already involved”

“see - what a beautiful picture”

Amanda froze.

The implication settled in — slow, suffocating. Her fingerprints. Her face. The syringe in her hand. Whatever this was, she was now part of it.

Her voice dropped to a whisper. “You’re insane.”

Zeke smiled — not wide, not cruel — just knowing.

“now help me out - carry this thing”

He bent down and grabbed the Dark Child by its legs. The hair shifted under his grip, reacting like it was still aware, still listening.

Amanda stared.

Up close, it was worse. The hair wasn’t just hair — it moved in slow, nervous ripples, like it remembered fear even while unconscious. No mouth. No face. Just eyes that refused to fully close.

“What… is it?” she breathed. “Is it— alive?”

Zeke didn’t answer.

He only smiled again.

Her hands trembled as she crouched, grabbing the other end, flinching when the hair brushed her wrist. Cold seeped into her skin immediately, a hollow chill that felt like standing too close to someone else’s grief.

They moved through the woods in silence.

The forest seemed to lean inward as they passed, branches creaking softly, shadows stretching and recoiling as if they recognized the thing being carried. Amanda’s breath fogged in the air despite the season.

Then Zeke slowed.

He tilted his head slightly.

Someone was there.

Not close — but not far either.

A girl sat beneath a tree, knees pulled to her chest, hair falling forward as she stared at the ground. She hadn’t noticed them. She was still. Too still. Like someone waiting for a thought to finish breaking them.

Moonlight caught her profile for a brief second.

Zeke stopped walking.

The Dark Child dragged slightly against the ground as his grip loosened.

His eyes stayed on the girl.

Interest sparked — quiet, precise. Not hunger. Not desire.

Recognition.

Amanda followed his gaze and swallowed. “Zeke… we should go.”

He didn’t respond.

The girl shifted, hugging herself tighter, unaware she was being observed. The night seemed to fold around her, protective and predatory all at once.

Zeke’s smile faded into something sharper.



Something thoughtful.

Then, without a word, he resumed walking.

The woods swallowed them again — but the image of the girl lingered in his mind, etched deeper than the bodies, deeper than the Dark Child.
