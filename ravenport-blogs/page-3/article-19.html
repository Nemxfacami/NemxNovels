The Bird Who Healed Other Birds

RAVENPORT TRENDS
Share:

There is always one.

The person who notices when everyone else is falling apart.

The one who stays awake until someone feels better.

The one who listens without asking for anything in return.

They don't seek much.

They don't need grand gestures or endless praise. Sometimes, all they want is to know that someone is okay.

So they become a place where broken people can rest.

Like a tree in the middle of a storm, they let others cling to them until the weather passes.

And somehow, the birds always find them.

The lonely ones.

The frightened ones.

The ones with broken wings.

The ones who have forgotten how to fly.

They come because there is something gentle about this person.

Something safe.

Something that says, You can stay here for a while.

And they do.

They tell their stories.

They cry.

They confess the things they have never told anyone.

And the healer listens.

They help them put the pieces back together.

They remind them that they are worth loving.

They convince them that tomorrow might still be worth seeing.

And slowly, the birds heal.

Their wings become stronger.

They begin to fly again.

The healer watches them leave with a smile.

And that smile is genuine.

Because seeing someone else become happy makes them feel alive.

For a little while, they forget their own wounds.

They forget the cracks underneath their feathers.

They forget that they, too, came into this world with a broken wing.

The Part Nobody Notices

The strange thing about people who heal others is that everyone assumes they are already healed.

Nobody asks what happens when they go home.

Nobody asks who listens to them.

Nobody asks who notices when their smile disappears.

Everyone knows how to find them when they are hurting.

Almost nobody knows how to find them when the healer is the one who needs help.

So they keep giving.

They keep listening.

They keep saying,

"You'll be okay."

Even when they aren't sure they will be.

They become so good at carrying other people's pain that they forget how heavy their own has become.

And perhaps that is the cruelest part.

They don't always help others because they have everything figured out.

Sometimes they help because they know exactly what it feels like to be the person nobody came to save.

A Broken Bird Among Broken Birds

Eventually, the healer realizes something.

They were never a bird sitting safely above the others.

They were one of them.

A broken bird pretending it knew how to fly.

Perhaps that is why they understood everyone so well.

They recognized the pain.

They recognized the silence.

They recognized the look someone gives when they are desperately hoping somebody notices.

They knew where the wounds were because they had wounds in the same places.

And every time they healed someone else, something inside them briefly felt alive.

For a moment, their own emptiness became quiet.

For a moment, they weren't the person who needed saving.

They were the person doing the saving.

And that gave them purpose.

Then the Birds Leave

But healed birds eventually fly away.

That's what healing is supposed to mean.

They find someone.

They find somewhere.

They find a life.

And the healer is happy for them.

At least, they try to be.

They watch the birds disappear into the distance, knowing they helped them get there.

Then the sky becomes quiet again.

The branches become empty.

And the healer is alone.

Again.

Their own wounds are still there.

Their feathers are still damaged.

Their wings still ache.

Nothing was ever repaired.

They simply became distracted by someone else's pain.

So they wait.

They sit on the same branch.

They look toward the sky.

And eventually, they hear wings again.

Another broken bird.

Another wounded soul.

Another person who needs somewhere safe to land.

And the healer opens their wings.

Not because they aren't tired.

Not because they aren't broken.

But because helping someone else makes them feel alive.

So they do it again.

And again.

And again.

Perhaps the Healer Needs Healing Too

There is something beautiful about people who care for others.

But there is something tragic about believing your only purpose is to save everyone else.

You are allowed to need someone.

You are allowed to be tired.

You are allowed to say, "I don't know how to fix this."

You are allowed to have broken wings.

You don't have to earn love by being useful.

You don't have to keep everyone else alive just to prove that you deserve to be here.

And you don't have to disappear into other people's pain to feel alive.

Maybe one day, the healer will stop waiting for another broken bird.

Maybe someone will finally look at them and say:

"You've been helping everyone else fly for so long. Who is helping you?"

And perhaps, for the first time, the healer won't know what to say.

Perhaps they'll finally let their wings fall.

Perhaps they'll finally allow someone else to sit beside them.

Not to fix them.

Not to owe them anything.

Just to stay.

Because sometimes the person who spends their entire life healing broken birds is the one who most needs someone willing to stay after their own healing begins.
