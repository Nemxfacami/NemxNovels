22 Avenue, Hatters Residence

The polished cream-and-gold marble floor gleamed beneath the warm lighting, partially covered by an ornate rug. Deep navy armchairs sat in the foreground, and a dark, gold-trimmed console stood against one wall, decorated with a bouquet of white flowers.

It was a beautiful room.

It was also missing a door.

Nobody had bothered replacing it, which was perhaps understandable. Some people preferred to wait until a storm had passed before repairing the roof.

Her eyes went straight to the bed.

It was difficult to tell whether he found her honesty amusing or whether he had simply grown accustomed to the peculiarities of the people he employed. Either way, he seemed remarkably comfortable with the prospect of his own murder.

“Raymond is going to deal with him this coming Tuesday. He had problems with the Doyleys.”

After inspecting several candidates, he found one with a little wine remaining and took a sip.

“Come,” he said, gesturing towards himself.

Lucille rolled her eyes but walked over.

“Drink with me.”

Raymond had finished bathing and was dressed for the afternoon.

Troysand Beach

An hour and fifty minutes later, Raymond had parked his car in the parking lot at the top of the hill.

Below him lay Troysand Beach, stretching along the coast beneath the afternoon sky. The ocean moved in long, unhurried waves, each one rolling towards the shore before collapsing into white foam and retreating. The breeze carried the smell of saltwater, and the sand shifted beneath Raymond's bare feet as he walked along the beach.

He was alone.

There was something peaceful about the absence of people. No conversations to maintain, no questions to answer, and no one demanding that he explain himself. Just the ocean, the wind, and the occasional cry of a seabird somewhere overhead.

Raymond admired the water as he walked, his hands resting comfortably in his pockets.

He had chosen the place for a reason. Troysand Beach was his favourite spot in Kingsmere whenever he had things to think about or words he needed to say. The ocean had a way of making problems feel smaller, even when it did absolutely nothing to solve them.

Unfortunately, he had invited Arabella Cavendish to join him.

And Arabella was not the sort of person who made problems feel smaller.

Up in the parking lot, Arabella had spotted his car.

She approached it, expecting to find Raymond inside. When she discovered the vehicle was empty, she looked around, her expression briefly clouded by confusion. Then she glanced down the hill.

There he was, walking along the beach, apparently admiring the ocean.

Arabella smiled.

She made her way towards the stairs leading down to the sand, careful not to lose her balance in her high heels. By the time she reached the bottom, Raymond had noticed her and was already walking towards her.

“You made it. I thought you'd be late,” he said.

“Oh, come on. I wouldn't make you wait too long.”

Raymond looked down at her shoes.

“Let me help with those.”

He crouched in front of her and gently removed her high heels.

“Barefoot?” Arabella asked, looking at him with mild surprise.

“It's nice. You'll love it, trust me.”

“I trust you.”

She smiled at him.

Raymond looked up, momentarily caught off guard by the sincerity in her expression. For someone who had spent much of her life surrounded by complicated people and complicated arrangements, Arabella could make a simple smile feel like an agreement to something neither party had properly discussed.

“I care about your interests,” she said. “Please, don't be quiet.”

Raymond stared at her for a moment, confused by the sudden declaration.

Arabella flipped her hair over her shoulder, still smiling as though she had said something perfectly ordinary.

He rose to his feet, took her hand, and led her down towards the water.

The sand was cool beneath their feet. A breeze swept across the beach, stirring Arabella's hair while the waves continued their steady approach and retreat.

“The beach is the most peaceful place in Kingsmere,” Raymond said. “It's my favourite place when I have things to say.”

“I thought the cemetery was the most peaceful.”

Raymond kept his eyes on the ocean.

He was beginning to suspect that bringing Arabella here had been a bad idea. Unfortunately, he had already committed himself to the date, and there were few things more exhausting than trying to escape a situation one had willingly arranged.

“I didn't know your father was Hugo Cavendish,” he said.

“Yes, he is my father. He's a lovely father at times. What about him?”

Arabella's smile remained in place, bright and almost unnaturally enduring. She looked at Raymond with the fondness of a woman who had been waiting for an opportunity to spend time with him, rather than someone who had recently left a basement where a man had died.

“Do you perhaps know when he'll be free?” Raymond asked.

“I do. I think he said Monday. He'll be resting, or something about a cigar being delivered.”

Raymond absorbed the information without changing his expression.

Monday.

He filed the detail away in his mind, careful not to let his interest show too plainly.

“But we're on a date,” Arabella continued. “Shouldn't we be talking about love and stuff?”

“We should, but I was just asking questions. Being curious, that's all.”

“Oh. Oh, oh, oh.”

Arabella's expression shifted as the explanation seemed to settle in her mind.

“I give a fuck,” she announced. “I give lots of fucks. Actually, I'm a prostitute of feelings and caring about what you're curious about.”

Raymond looked at her.

There were moments in life when a person could do little more than accept the words presented to them and hope that time would eventually make sense of everything. This was one of those moments.

He had questions about Hugo. He had questions about Monday. He had questions about several matters that could potentially get him killed.

And now, apparently, he had a woman who was prepared to prostitute her feelings in exchange for the privilege of answering them.

“I understand, Bella,” he said.

Arabella smiled again and touched his shoulder.

“Let's go find something to eat,” Raymond suggested.

“I'm very hungry. I didn't eat.”

“I'll treat you well, then.”

They walked back towards the parking lot, Arabella holding tightly onto Raymond's arm as though she feared he might suddenly change his mind and disappear into the ocean.

Raymond didn't comment on her grip.

He had more important things to worry about, although he was beginning to suspect that Arabella herself might eventually qualify as one of them.

Once they reached the car, he opened the passenger door for her. She climbed inside, placing her high heels beside her before settling into the seat. Raymond walked around to the driver's side and got in.

For the first time that afternoon, they were heading somewhere that resembled an ordinary date.

The car pulled out of the parking lot and began its journey towards the town.

“I heard you're a lawyer,” Raymond said as they drove. “Besides being a lawyer, what do you like doing?”

Arabella turned towards him.

“I am naturally good at doing what I'm not supposed to be doing.”

Raymond glanced at her.

“I've noticed.”

“Really?”

“Really. I've noticed it many times.”

Arabella's smile widened.

Raymond returned his attention to the road.

He had come to Troysand Beach hoping for a peaceful walk and a chance to ask a few questions. Instead, he had found himself holding hands with Hugo Cavendish's daughter, listening to her discuss the prostitution of feelings, and preparing to share a meal with a woman whose definition of an ordinary afternoon was probably best kept away from the authorities.

Still, the date had only just begun.

There was plenty of time for things to get worse.
