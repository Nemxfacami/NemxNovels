He opened the door with one hand, slow and unhurried, the way a man does when he already knows who is on the other side.

He wore a three-piece suit in charcoal grey wool, finely woven and cut close to his frame. The jacket had wide notched lapels, and a crimson silk handkerchief was folded into the breast pocket, the only splash of colour on him. Under the jacket sat a matching waistcoat, buttoned high, with a thin gold chain looping across the front toward a pocket watch, a small tassel hanging from it.

His shirt was pale with a stiff white collar, and at his throat he wore a dark grey patterned silk tie, knotted tight and fixed with a small pin. His dark hair was slicked back from his forehead, silvering at the temples, and his thin moustache was neatly kept.

His expression gave nothing away. His brows pulled together slightly, his lips were pressed flat, and his eyes settled on the visitor with cool, measured suspicion.

Behind him, the hallway was dim, lit by warm lamplight that caught the gold of the watch chain and the red of the handkerchief.

He stood in front of the gym door like a man who had no intention of going in yet. A long, weathered charcoal coat hung off his shoulders, the leather creased at the elbows and faded at the seams, with its collar turned up against his jaw. Beneath it, a dark grey shirt was buttoned most of the way up, plain and unfussy, the kind of shirt that hid wrinkles and sweat equally well.

His trousers were black suit trousers, sharply creased and out of place against the gym's chipped paint and cracked pavement. Below them, heavy black lace-up boots, scuffed at the toes, with one lace hanging loose as if he'd never thought it worth tying.

Black leather gloves covered his hands. In one, he carried a black plastic bag, hanging low and heavy, its contents hidden. The other hand stayed tucked into his coat pocket.

His dark, curly hair was messy, and his thick beard was trimmed just enough to look deliberate. His brow was low and his stare was fixed on the door, with the flat, unimpressed scowl of someone who had already decided how the next few minutes would go. He didn't fidget or check his surroundings. He just stood there, patient and still.

He stood at the balcony rail in the pale hush of early morning, gazing out at the mountains as the first light crept along their ridges.

On his head sat a wide-brimmed black hat with a flat crown, tilted just low enough to shade his eyes. A long black coat hung from his shoulders, cut in a formal, almost clerical style, its fabric heavy and slightly rumpled from the night. Draped around his neck was a white silk scarf, the two ends falling long down his chest, finished with a grey fringe that stirred in the cold mountain breeze. Beneath the coat, the collar of a pale shirt showed open at the throat, worn loose and without a tie.

Both hands rested on the top of a black cane planted in front of him, fingers laced together. Several heavy rings sat on them, gold and silver, catching the early light whenever he shifted his grip. His thick, reddish-brown beard was untrimmed at the edges, and his gaze stayed fixed on the peaks, distant and unreadable, as if the view were something he was weighing rather than admiring.

Behind him, the sitting room lay dim and quiet, with the faint smell of last night's fire still hanging in the air.
