Dear diarrry,<br><br><br>
Melissa asked me something today.<br><br>
She asked me how I know so much about her.<br><br>
I didn't know what to say.<br><br>
So I told her I pay attention.<br><br>
That seemed to make her happy.<br><br>
I don't think people mind being watched when they believe the person watching them cares.<br><br>
At work, we have a few methods we use with subjects.<br><br>
I won't write the names of them.<br><br>
There is no reason to.<br><br>
Some things should probably stay at work.<br><br>
But there is one thing I wanted to try.<br><br>
Statements.<br><br>
We use them sometimes when we want a subject to tell us something without asking them directly.<br><br>
You say something that sounds personal.<br><br>
Something that could be true.<br><br>
Then you wait.<br><br>
People are strange.<br><br>
They don't always tell you whether you're wrong.<br><br>
Sometimes they explain why you're right.<br><br>
So I tried it with Melissa.<br><br>
I told her:<br><br>
"You don't trust people easily."<br><br>
She looked at me and said that wasn't true.<br><br>
She trusts people.<br><br>
Then she told me about the last three people she stopped talking to.<br><br>
She told me why she stopped talking to them.<br><br>
She told me what they did.<br><br>
She told me how it made her feel.<br><br>
I didn't ask anything.<br><br>
I just listened.<br><br>
So I tried again.<br><br>
"You trust people when they prove that they deserve it."<br><br>
She smiled.<br><br>
She said:<br><br>
"Exactly."<br><br>
I wrote that one down.<br><br>
Same person.<br><br>
Same idea.<br><br>
Different sentence.<br><br>
I think that's important.<br><br>
Then I tried another.<br><br>
"You like being around people, but sometimes you need to be alone."<br><br>
She laughed.<br><br>
She said that was exactly her.<br><br>
I almost laughed too.<br><br>
Of course it was.<br><br>
Who doesn't want to be alone sometimes?<br><br>
But Melissa didn't hear it like that.<br><br>
She heard it as me knowing something about her.<br><br>
That was when I became interested.<br><br>
So I kept going.<br><br>
"You care about people more than you let them know."<br><br>
She stopped eating.<br><br>
She asked me how I knew.<br><br>
I said I could tell.<br><br>
She smiled.<br><br>
Then she started telling me about her mother.<br><br>
I didn't know about her mother.<br><br>
Now I do.<br><br>
I tried another.<br><br>
"Sometimes you pretend something doesn't bother you because you don't want people to know that it does."<br><br>
She went quiet.<br><br>
Then she told me about a man she used to know.<br><br>
I had never heard of him before.<br><br>
Now I know his name.<br><br>
I know where he worked.<br><br>
I know why she hated him.<br><br>
I know why she still thinks about him.<br><br>
All from one sentence.<br><br>
I think I understand something now.<br><br>
The statement doesn't have to be true.<br><br>
It only has to feel like it could be true.<br><br>
The person hearing it does the rest.<br><br>
That is what we learned from the subjects.<br><br>
I always thought it was because subjects were different.<br><br>
Maybe they aren't.<br><br>
Maybe people are people.<br><br>
I tried something more difficult.<br><br>
I told Melissa:<br><br>
"You don't like hurting people, but sometimes you become angry enough that you imagine what you would do if someone pushed you too far."<br><br>
She stared at me.<br><br>
Then she asked:<br><br>
"Do I really seem like that?"<br><br>
I told her no.<br><br>
She seemed relieved.<br><br>
Then she started explaining why she would never hurt anyone.<br><br>
She talked for almost ten minutes.<br><br>
I didn't interrupt her once.<br><br>
It was fascinating.<br><br>
She wasn't answering me anymore.<br><br>
She was answering herself.<br><br>
I think that might be the trick.<br><br>
You give someone a door.<br><br>
They decide what's behind it.<br><br>
Sometimes they even build the room themselves.<br><br>
I tried one last statement before we left.<br><br>
"You want people to understand you, but you're afraid they might understand you too well."<br><br>
Melissa didn't laugh this time.<br><br>
She just looked at me.<br><br>
Then she asked:<br><br>
"Do you understand me?"<br><br>
I said yes.<br><br>
She smiled.<br><br>
She held my hand.<br><br>
I felt bad.<br><br>
Not because I lied.<br><br>
I don't think I lied.<br><br>
I understand her now.<br><br>
Just not because I'm good at understanding people.<br><br>
She taught me.<br><br>
She doesn't know that.<br><br>
I think that is the most interesting part.<br><br>
Tomorrow I want to try something else.<br><br>
Something about jealousy.<br><br>
Dave has been spending more time around her lately.<br><br>
I wonder what she would say if I told her:<br><br>
"Sometimes you mistake attention for affection."<br><br>
Maybe she will tell me I'm wrong.<br><br>
Maybe she'll tell me who she thinks actually loves her.<br><br>
Maybe she'll tell me about Dave.<br><br>
Maybe she'll tell me about me.<br><br>
I should stop writing.<br><br>
It's late.<br><br>
Melissa just sent me a message.<br><br>
She said she has something important to tell me tomorrow.<br><br>
I wonder what it is.<br><br>
Goodnight, Diary.<br><br>
I think the experiment is working.<br><br>
