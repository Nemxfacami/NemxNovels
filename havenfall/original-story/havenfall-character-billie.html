Personality

Traits

  • Emotionally vulnerable and strongly affected by abandonment.
  • Craves reassurance and fears being left behind.
  • Forms deep attachments and can become dependent on people who provide emotional security.
  • Socially capable but becomes uncertain when confronted with powerful or unfamiliar people.
  • Apologizes frequently when she believes she has disappointed someone.
  • Sensitive to changes in other people's moods and behavior.
  • Wants to belong, particularly when entering unfamiliar social environments.
  • Can be affectionate and playful when she feels comfortable.
  • Easily influenced by people she trusts or perceives as safe.
  • Has a strong desire to be loved and reassured.
  • Can temporarily suppress discomfort to preserve a relationship.
  • Becomes increasingly passive when surrounded by stronger personalities.
  • Even after frightening experiences, she tends to seek familiarity rather than distance herself from it.


Flaws

  • Has difficulty establishing boundaries with people she is emotionally attached to.
  • Can mistake possessiveness for affection or security.
  • Fear of abandonment makes her vulnerable to manipulation.
  • Often apologizes instead of confronting the person who has hurt or frightened her.
  • Can prioritize another person's expectations over her own safety.
  • Allows people with stronger personalities to determine the direction of situations.
  • Her need for connection can override her ability to recognize danger.
  • Becomes particularly vulnerable when she is frightened, confused, or chemically affected.
  • Has difficulty distinguishing what she genuinely wants from what she has been conditioned or influenced to want.
  • Her attachment to people can become stronger than her trust in her own perceptions.


Motivations

  • To feel loved and wanted.
  • To maintain the relationships that make her feel secure.
  • To avoid being abandoned.
  • To remain connected to Matt despite their increasingly unhealthy relationship.
  • To gain acceptance from the people around Matt and the Mathens family.
  • To understand what has happened to her.
  • Later, seemingly, to reach Andrew—even though the origin of that desire becomes increasingly unclear.


Hates

  • Being ignored or rejected.
  • Feeling like someone is leaving her.
  • Conflict with people she loves.
  • Feeling unwanted or excluded.
  • Being treated as though she doesn't belong.
  • Losing control of her own circumstances.
  • Being unable to understand why she is behaving strangely.
  • Being separated from people she has emotionally attached herself to.


Strengths

  • Strong capacity for emotional attachment.
  • Capable of showing affection openly.
  • Willing to enter unfamiliar environments for people she cares about.
  • Socially adaptable when she feels secure.
  • Able to recover physically and emotionally from situations that should be overwhelming.
  • Has enough independence to act on her own when she believes something matters.
  • Her observations and questions can occasionally contribute meaningfully to investigations—for example, her question about where Martin studied his conspiracy theories redirects the Mathens family's attention toward his locations and associates.
  • Despite her vulnerability, she continues moving forward rather than completely withdrawing from the world.


Fears

  • Being abandoned.
  • Losing the people she loves.
  • Being unwanted.
  • Being excluded from someone's life.
  • Being unable to control what happens to her.
  • Discovering that her feelings aren't actually her own.
  • Being unable to trust her own memories or behavior.
  • Becoming separated from the person she believes can make her feel safe.


Relationships

  • Matt Mathens — Her romantic partner. Their relationship is possessive and complicated. Matt calls her “my property,” controls aspects of her appearance and behavior, and becomes protective of her. Billie nevertheless seeks his approval and reassurance. Their relationship becomes even stranger after her encounter with the Mathens family and her transformation-like episode.
  • Cyrus Karmen — Cyrus's former romantic attachment. Their relationship ends after Billie cheats on him with Matt. Her betrayal becomes one of the major emotional events shaping Cyrus's detachment.
  • Andrew Mathens — Initially Matt's older brother, but his significance to Billie changes dramatically. After Mavis's green-smoke incident, Billie wakes calling Andrew's name affectionately, travels toward him, and later kisses and injects him with a syringe. The exact nature of her attachment to Andrew remains unexplained.
  • Mavis Mathens — Treats Billie warmly and quickly includes her within the family environment, but later gives Billie the mysterious green substance that leaves her unconscious. Mavis describes it as something intended to help Billie “relax.”
  • Beatrice Whitmore — Matt's butler and one of the people responsible for looking after Billie after her collapse. Beatrice treats her as someone under Matt's protection and monitors her condition.
  • The Mathens Family — Billie is an outsider attempting to enter an extremely established family structure. Andrew welcomes her; Mavis embraces her; Belladonna evaluates her; Matt tries to control her presence; the guards initially refuse her entry. Her position within the family is therefore constantly shifting.
  • Emily — No direct relationship established yet, but both characters become involved in the strange mechanisms surrounding Havenfall, the Hole, and the Edenus Foundation.

Backstory

Billie's early appearances establish her as someone deeply affected by attachment and abandonment. Cyrus first encounters her alone and distressed, and their conversations gradually become a routine. The routine develops into emotional dependency, eventually becoming a relationship.

That relationship collapses when Billie cheats on Cyrus with Matt Mathens. Even after forgetting Cyrus's birthday and betraying him, she continues moving through relationships in search of security and belonging.

With Matt, Billie enters a considerably more complicated environment. Matt is possessive and socially powerful, while Billie is frequently uncertain about how she fits into his world. She nevertheless becomes increasingly involved with the Mathens family after Andrew personally invites her to their gathering.

At the Mathens estate, Billie is initially treated as an outsider. Andrew, however, immediately welcomes her, calls her “lil sis,” feeds her, and invites her to return. His acceptance contrasts sharply with the family's formal hierarchy and Matt's discomfort.

The situation changes after Mavis gives Billie the mysterious green substance. Billie later wakes with an apparently normal body and normal medical results, yet something about her behavior has changed. She escapes Matt's residence, creates a fake sleeping body, injures herself jumping from a second-floor window, and repeatedly follows the path while calling “Andrew.”

Later, she appears in Martin's underground facility in a pale, weakened state. Matt carries her back to Victor's estate, where medical examinations reveal no obvious physical or psychological explanation for what happened.

Then her behavior becomes even stranger.

She kisses Andrew and injects him with a syringe she cannot explain possessing. She is capable of lifting him effortlessly despite having recently appeared physically weak. Yet afterward she behaves toward Matt almost exactly as she did before, asking for a date, worrying about him, and telling him that only he can heal her.

This creates one of Billie's central mysteries:

Is Billie changing, or is something using Billie while leaving her original personality intact?

The story deliberately hasn't answered that yet.



Trivia

Billie's relationship with Cyrus and her relationship with Matt create an important contrast in the story: Cyrus observes her emotionally, while Matt attempts to possess and control her.

Her repeated use of “Andrew” is particularly significant because it occurs without a clear explanation of why she suddenly becomes attached to him.

Her body also behaves strangely. She survives the second-floor jump, later appears physically altered, carries Andrew with apparently impossible strength, and yet Dr. Zen's examinations find nothing medically abnormal.

One of the biggest clues surrounding Billie is that her conscious explanations don't match her actions. She doesn't know where the syringe came from. She doesn't know why she injected Andrew. She doesn't know why she escaped. She simply describes these actions as feeling “right.”

That makes Billie less like a conventional supernatural character and more like a character caught between what she wants, what she remembers, and what something else wants her to do.
