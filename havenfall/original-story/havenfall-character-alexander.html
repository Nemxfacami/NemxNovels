Basic Profile

Age : 56

Affiliation : Edenus Foundation

Status : Alive

Residence : Edenus Foundation, Havenfall

Family : Emily Stiles, Ethan Stiles, Levi Stiles

Role : Foundation leader / spiritual authority / gardener; senior Edenus Foundation figure; Emily's father; participant in the preparation of the Chosen One



Personality

Traits

  • Calm — Rarely appears rushed or emotionally disturbed, even when discussing disturbing events.
  • Patient — His gardening reflects the same patience he applies to people and situations.
  • Gentle in appearance — His interactions with Emily and the congregation are affectionate and peaceful.
  • Authoritative — People within the Foundation clearly recognize his position.
  • Observant — He understands developments surrounding Emily before she herself appears to understand them.
  • Faith-driven — His worldview is deeply connected to belief, suffering, sacrifice, and divine purpose.
  • Manipulative — He deliberately allows Emily to suffer because he believes suffering is necessary for her transformation.
  • Methodical — He treats the development of the Chosen One almost like a carefully managed process.
  • Certain — Alexander rarely speaks as though the future is uncertain. His visions give him confidence in what is coming.
  • Symbolic thinker — He explains complicated ideas through seeds, flowers, darkness, soil, and growth.
  • Protective in his own way — He believes he is preparing Emily for something greater, although his methods are deeply questionable.
  • Ritualistic — His world revolves around preparation, faith, ceremonies, waiting, and predetermined events.


Flaws

  • Justifies suffering — Alexander believes suffering can be necessary for growth, allowing him to rationalize what happens to Emily.
  • Manipulates faith — He deliberately creates circumstances where the Chosen One's faith can be tested.
  • Treats people as part of a process — Instead of simply protecting Emily, he views her development as something that must be cultivated.
  • Excessive certainty — His belief in the vision leaves little room for questioning whether the path itself is right.
  • Withholds information — He knows considerably more about what is happening than he tells others.
  • Detached morality — His philosophy allows terrible events to continue because they serve a perceived greater purpose.
  • Controls through ideology — He does not necessarily need physical force; belief itself becomes a mechanism of control.
  • Prioritizes destiny over individual choice — His statement that the Chosen One was never supposed to have complete freedom reveals how little he values ordinary agency when it conflicts with the prophecy.


Motivations

  • Prepare the Chosen One for what is coming.
  • Ensure Emily's faith survives increasingly severe trials.
  • Fulfill the vision he has received.
  • Prepare the Edenus Foundation for the coming event.
  • Unite the necessary people and circumstances.
  • Preserve the Foundation's beliefs and purpose.
  • See the prophecy reach its intended completion.
  • Cultivate Emily into the person the Foundation believes she must become.
  • Alexander's most important motivation appears to be fulfillment of the vision. He doesn't seem to view Emily's suffering as an unfortunate side effect. He views it as part of the process.


Hates

  • Not explicitly established, but his behavior suggests a strong opposition to:
  • Faithlessness.
  • Abandoning one's purpose.
  • Interference with the Foundation's plans.
  • Removing someone from the process before their transformation is complete.
  • Resistance that prevents what he considers necessary growth.
  • His philosophy suggests that he may not actually hate resistance itself. In fact, he appears to expect it.


Strengths

  • Leadership — Commands respect within the Edenus Foundation.
  • Patience — Willing to wait for events to unfold rather than forcing everything immediately.
  • Psychological understanding — Understands how suffering, faith, hope, and uncertainty affect people.
  • Manipulation — Can influence people without appearing overtly threatening.
  • Conviction — His belief in the vision appears extremely strong.
  • Symbolic reasoning — Uses simple natural imagery to communicate complicated ideas.
  • Composure — Maintains a peaceful exterior even when discussing disturbing subjects.
  • Knowledge — Clearly understands aspects of the Foundation and its prophecy that other characters do not.
  • Social authority — His position gives his words considerable weight.
  • Long-term planning — His actions indicate that the Foundation's plans have existed for considerably longer than Emily's current experiences.


Fears

  • Alexander's personal fears are not directly established, but the story suggests several possibilities without confirming them:
  • The vision failing.
  • The Chosen One abandoning faith.
  • The Foundation being unprepared when the predicted event arrives.
  • Someone interfering with Emily's transformation.
  • The future shown by the vision not occurring as expected.
  • Interestingly, Alexander doesn't behave like someone who is frightened by the supernatural. He behaves like someone who has already accepted it.


Relationships

  • Emily Stiles — Daughter / Chosen One. Alexander appears genuinely affectionate toward Emily as her father. However, their relationship becomes increasingly complicated because he also sees her as the Chosen One. He knows she is being subjected to suffering and does not intervene because he believes the suffering is necessary. His relationship with Emily therefore exists on two levels: Father → daughter and Foundation leader → Chosen One. Those two roles may eventually come into conflict.
  • Ethan Stiles — Son. Alexander is Ethan's father, although their relationship has received less attention than his relationship with Emily. Ethan appears to exist within the ordinary family environment surrounding the Foundation.
  • Levi Stiles — Son. Alexander is Levi's father. Their relationship has not yet been explored deeply.
  • Anthony Matthews — Foundation associate. Alexander clearly considers Anthony useful to the Foundation's larger plan. When Anthony admits that he has witnessed things he cannot explain, Alexander doesn't dismiss him. Instead, he confirms that what Anthony has experienced corresponds with the vision. Alexander also tells Anthony that the vision showed someone would arrive who would help prepare what comes next.
  • Edenus Foundation — Alexander appears to hold a significant position within the Foundation. The congregation treats the Foundation's rituals and teachings with absolute seriousness, while Alexander operates behind the peaceful surface as someone who understands the larger purpose.


Hobbies

  • Gardening — Alexander is shown carefully tending white lilies. But gardening is more than a hobby for him. It is practically a philosophical language.
  • He uses the growth of a flower to explain suffering: A seed struggles beneath the soil before it can reach the light.
  • He applies the same principle to Emily. The garden therefore reflects Alexander himself: peaceful, patient, beautiful on the surface, but built around a controlled process of growth.


Backstory

Very little of Alexander's personal history has been revealed.

What is known is that he became deeply involved with the Edenus Foundation and its beliefs.

At some point, Alexander experienced or received a vision concerning the future.

The vision apparently revealed that someone would become important to what was coming and that the Chosen One would need to be cultivated rather than simply discovered.

Emily appears to be that Chosen One.

Alexander therefore allows events to happen that would normally be considered horrifying or unacceptable because he believes they are necessary trials.

His philosophy is explicitly revealed when he compares Emily to a seed.

A seed must remain buried, struggle, endure darkness, and survive storms before it reaches the light.

Alexander believes the same must happen to Emily.

He therefore does not attempt to prevent every terrible thing happening to her.

Instead, he watches the process.

His greatest revelation is that the Chosen One cannot simply be found.

She must be cultivated.

She must suffer.

She must have opportunities to abandon her faith.

And if she continues believing despite those experiences, Alexander considers her worthy.

This makes Alexander one of the clearest links between Emily's personal story and the larger Edenus Foundation mystery.



Trivia

  • Alexander is introduced through family warmth and gardening, rather than obvious menace.
  • His gardening becomes a metaphor for his entire philosophy.
  • He believes suffering can be necessary for transformation.
  • He explicitly states that the Chosen One was never supposed to simply be found.
  • He knows about Emily's journey while Emily herself doesn't understand the larger picture.
  • His calmness makes his philosophy more unsettling because he discusses extreme suffering without appearing disturbed by it.
  • The Foundation's peaceful environment contrasts sharply with what Alexander allows to happen.
  • He refers to the people he has gathered as “the unclean.”
  • His visions appear to influence major decisions within the Foundation.
  • Alexander doesn't seem interested in rushing destiny. His philosophy is based on cultivation, patience, and preparation.
  • His most important contradiction is that he can genuinely love Emily as a father while simultaneously treating her suffering as a necessary experiment in faith.
  • The white lilies he tends mirror Emily's transformation: something growing toward beauty while passing through darkness.
  • His philosophy makes the distinction between faith and free will increasingly important to the story.
  • Alexander may not consider himself cruel at all. From his perspective, allowing suffering may be an act of preparation rather than punishment.
  • His greatest power may not be physical or supernatural. It is his ability to make terrible things sound necessary.
