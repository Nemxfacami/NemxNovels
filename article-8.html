The Unwritten Rules of Western Ravenport University

Western Ravenport University has rules.

A lot of them, actually.

Some are printed on walls, some are buried in student handbooks, and some are repeated by lecturers every semester.

But then there are the rules nobody officially wrote down.

The rules students learn from other students.

And if you're new here, you should probably learn them quickly.

1. Don't Wander Around Too Much

Western Ravenport University is bigger than it looks.

You might think you've figured out where everything is after your first week, then somehow end up in a corridor you've never seen before.

If you don't have somewhere specific to be, don't randomly explore the university.

Especially alone.

Getting lost isn't exactly difficult, and asking for directions isn't always as simple as you'd expect.

2. Remember Your Room Number Before You Leave

This sounds stupid until you actually need it.

Before leaving a room, remember the number.

Not the building.

Not the floor.

The room number.

Students who have been here long enough usually memorise the numbers of the rooms they regularly use.

New students tend to rely on their phones or simply assume they'll remember.

Don't.

You might need that number later.

3. If You Come Back and the Room Number Has Changed, Don't Go Inside

This is probably the most important rule.

If you leave a room and return later to find that its number has changed, do not enter.

Don't assume the university made a mistake.

Don't assume someone replaced the sign.

And definitely don't walk inside just to see what's going on.

Report it to one of the guards.

They'll deal with it.

Students have different theories about why this happens, but nobody seems particularly interested in testing them.

4. If You're New, Ask Someone for the Room Number

Don't be embarrassed to ask.

Seriously.

If you're standing around looking confused, another student will probably understand what's happening before you explain.

Ask someone where the room is.

Most students are willing to help, especially if you're obviously new.

Just remember the number once they give it to you.

You'll probably need it again.

5. Don't Sell Room Numbers

Yes.

People actually sell them.

Room numbers are useful, especially to students who know how to navigate the university properly, and there are people who make a little money by keeping track of them.

But don't assume you can just start doing the same thing.

Room numbers are usually exchanged between close friends, trusted students, or people who already deal in them.

Trying to become a dealer yourself can get you into trouble with people who were already doing it long before you arrived.

So unless you're looking for unnecessary problems, leave the business alone.

6. Room Numbers Are Personal

Don't go around collecting room numbers from everyone.

Students exchange them with close friends.

Sometimes they exchange them with dealers.

Sometimes someone gives you one because they trust you.

That doesn't mean you should immediately start passing it around.

A room number might seem like nothing more than a number.

At Western Ravenport University, students treat some of them like private information.

And honestly, after you've been here long enough, you'll understand why.

Final Advice

Western Ravenport University isn't necessarily difficult to understand.

You just have to pay attention.

Learn where you're going.

Remember your room numbers.

Ask when you don't know something.

And if something changes that shouldn't have changed, don't investigate it yourself.

There are plenty of things at university you can learn through experience.

That probably isn't one of them.
