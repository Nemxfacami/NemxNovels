DON’T BE MISERABLE IN ADVANCE

Havenfall Gossips
Share:

Perspective on Waiting for the Worst

There is a particular kind of misery that Havenfall knows very well.

It is the misery of waiting.

Waiting for the phone to ring.

Waiting for an answer.

Waiting for an exam result, a doctor's decision, a letter, a confession, a rejection, or news that might change everything.

Nothing has happened yet.

And somehow, we have already suffered through it.

This is one of the strangest habits of human beings: we experience tomorrow's pain today.

We imagine the worst possible outcome, then emotionally live through it before reality has even decided what will happen.

And when the actual news finally arrives, we may discover that none of it was necessary.

The Future Hasn't Happened

Imagine someone in Havenfall receives a message saying:

"We need to talk tomorrow."

That's it.

Four words.

Nothing more.

But the mind immediately begins working.

Did I do something wrong?

Am I in trouble?

Are they angry with me?

What if something happened?

What if everything changes tomorrow?

By midnight, the person has already constructed an entire disaster.

They have argued with imaginary people.

Lost imaginary opportunities.

Experienced imaginary heartbreak.

They have suffered through events that exist only inside their head.

And tomorrow hasn't even arrived.

This is what makes anxiety about the future so strange.

The event is uncertain.

The suffering is real.

But the suffering was created before the event ever occurred.

Havenfall's Waiting Rooms

Every city has places where people wait.

Hospitals.

Police stations.

Schools.

Government offices.

Train stations.

Courtrooms.

Even bedrooms at three in the morning.

Havenfall has plenty of them.

And waiting can make time feel cruel.

A person sitting outside a hospital room may stare at the same door for hours.

Someone waiting for an important message may check their phone every thirty seconds.

A student waiting for their results may refresh the same page until their fingers hurt.

The body remains in the present.

The mind has already travelled into the future.

And that is where the misery begins.

The Problem With Imaginary Suffering

There is nothing wrong with preparing for something.

Preparation is useful.

If something could go wrong, you can make a plan.

But preparation and suffering are not the same thing.

You can prepare for bad news without emotionally living inside it.

You can ask:

"What will I do if this happens?"

without constantly asking:

"What if this happens?"

The first question creates options.

The second can create an endless loop.

And the loop consumes the present.

A person can spend an entire afternoon worrying about tomorrow while completely missing the afternoon they actually have.

The Cruelest Part

Perhaps the cruelest part is that worrying doesn't guarantee anything.

If the news turns out to be good, you have wasted hours being miserable.

If the news turns out to be bad, you have experienced the pain twice:

Once in your imagination.

And once in reality.

The future receives no benefit from your suffering.

It simply steals the present.

The Busy Person

There is something powerful about having something meaningful to do while waiting.

Work.

Writing.

Studying.

Cleaning.

Building something.

Talking to someone.

Walking through Havenfall without constantly checking the phone.

Not because these things magically erase fear.

But because they remind you that your life is still happening.

The news has not arrived.

The world has not stopped.

You are still here.

There is still something you can do.

Let Tomorrow Arrive

Perhaps the healthiest response to uncertainty is surprisingly simple:

Let it come.

If the bad news arrives, deal with it then.

If the good news arrives, enjoy it then.

If nothing happens, you haven't wasted an entire day preparing yourself for a disaster that never existed.

There is courage in refusing to suffer prematurely.

Not because you believe everything will always work out.

But because you understand something more important:

You don't owe the future today's happiness.

Havenfall will continue moving.

People will walk to work.

Students will complain about classes.

Cars will pass through the streets.

Someone will fall in love.

Someone will lose something.

Someone will receive wonderful news.

Someone will receive terrible news.

And somewhere, someone will be sitting alone, staring at their phone, waiting for an answer.

To them, perhaps the best advice is this:

Put the phone down for a while.

Look around.

There is still a life happening in front of you.

Don't be miserable in advance.

Whatever tomorrow brings, let tomorrow carry it.

Today already has enough of its own.
