Nobody in Havenfall stays poor forever because, the Hole provides.

By Daniel Mercer
Share:

The Strange Case of a Kingsmere House

There is an old saying in Havenfall that most residents have heard at least once:

“The Hole provides when the time is right.”

For some, it is nothing more than an expression. For others, it is an unquestionable fact of life.

Havenfall has no shortage of people who have struggled financially, lost their homes, or found themselves with nowhere to turn. Yet, strangely enough, the city has almost no permanent homeless population.

The reason, according to those who have experienced it, is the Hole.

Last Tuesday, I was given a story that sounded so ridiculous that I initially assumed I had misunderstood the details.

A man had been homeless for less than twenty-four hours.

By Wednesday morning, he owned a house in Kingsmere.

His name is Jonathan Vale, a forty-six-year-old Havenfall resident who had spent most of his adult life living with his wife. According to Jonathan, she was the only person he considered family.

That changed Monday evening.

“I came home and she told me to leave,” Jonathan told me. “There wasn't a conversation. There wasn't an argument. She just said she didn't want me there anymore.” Jonathan left with the clothes he was wearing and a small bag containing his wallet and phone.

He had nowhere else to go.

“I remember sitting outside that night thinking, That's it. I had nobody to call.” Jonathan spent the night beneath the awning of a closed shop. He says he woke shortly after sunrise to find a small envelope beside him. Inside was a single key.

There was also an address.

Kingsmere.

Jonathan assumed it was some kind of cruel joke.

“I didn't even know the address,” he said. “I thought somebody was messing with me.” He went there anyway.

The house was real.

A two-story property with a small garden, functioning electricity, running water, furniture, food in the kitchen, and everything else one would expect from an occupied home.

Except nobody was occupying it.

There was no note explaining who owned the property.

No landlord.
No previous tenant.
No paperwork.
Only the key.

When I visited the house yesterday, Jonathan was already living there.

I asked him how he had obtained ownership of the property.

He looked at me for several seconds before answering.

“I didn't.”

“Then who gave it to you?”

Jonathan pointed toward the kitchen window.

“The Hole.”

That answer is becoming increasingly common in Havenfall.

Over the past several years, I have spoken with residents who claim to have received food, money, medicine, clothing, tools, jobs, and even vehicles under similarly unexplained circumstances.

The circumstances surrounding each case are different.

The timing, however, is remarkably similar.

People receive what they need when they are at their lowest.

Not before.

Not necessarily when they ask.

When the time is right.

Jonathan's case may be one of the strangest yet.

A man loses his home on Monday.

A house appears on Tuesday.

By Wednesday, he is no longer homeless.

There is no government program responsible.

No charity has claimed responsibility.

No property company has acknowledged transferring the house.

And Jonathan insists he never asked the Hole for anything.

“I didn't even think about it,” he said. “I just wanted somewhere to sleep.”

Before leaving, I asked Jonathan whether he believed the Hole had saved him.

He looked around his new home.

“I don't know if it saved me,” he said.

Then he paused.

“But it knew I needed somewhere to go.”

Perhaps that is what makes the Hole so difficult to understand.

It does not seem to answer prayers.

It does not seem to reward good people.

It does not appear to care about wealth, status, or reputation.

It simply waits.

And when the moment arrives, something appears.

For Jonathan Vale, that something was a house in Kingsmere.

For someone else, it may be something entirely different.

Perhaps that is why Havenfall has so few people sleeping on its streets.

Not because nobody falls.

But because, eventually—the Hole provides.
